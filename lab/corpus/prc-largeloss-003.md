# Large loss escalation procedure

A large loss is any claim where the total incurred cost is expected to exceed
£100,000, or any claim of any size that carries reputational, regulatory or
reinsurance consequences.

## Immediate steps

Within four working hours of identifying a large loss:

1. Notify the large loss mailbox with the claim reference and a one paragraph
   summary of what happened.
2. Set the large loss indicator on the claim record. This drives the reinsurance
   notification and the monthly board report, so it is not optional.
3. Confirm cover in principle, or record clearly that cover is under
   investigation. Do not tell the insured that cover is confirmed until it is.

## Ownership

Large losses are handled by the large loss unit, not by the originating team.
Handover happens within two working days and includes a written note of every
conversation held to that point.

## Reinsurance

Notify the reinsurance team where the expected cost exceeds fifty per cent of
the retention for that class. Retentions are set annually and are held in the
reinsurance treaty summary. Late notification can prejudice recovery under the
treaty, so notify on the estimate rather than waiting for certainty.

## Appointing suppliers

Loss adjusters, solicitors and forensic accountants on the panel may be
appointed by the large loss unit without further approval. Anyone not on the
panel requires the claims director's written approval, recorded on the file
before instruction.

## Communication

One named handler owns all contact with the insured, the broker and any third
party. Where the loss is likely to attract press interest, the communications
team is told before any statement is made and no other person comments.

## Reporting

Large losses are reported weekly to the claims director and monthly to the board
in summary form. The monthly report carries the reserve, the movement since the
previous report, the reason for the movement, and the expected settlement date.

## Review

Every closed large loss is reviewed within sixty days of closure. The review
asks what the claim cost, what it was first reserved at, why the two differ, and
what would have shortened the life of the claim. Findings feed the annual
revision of this procedure.
