# Subsidence claims guidance

Subsidence is downward movement of the ground beneath a building that is not
caused by the weight of the building itself. Heave is upward movement. Landslip
is movement down a slope. All three are handled under this guidance.

## First response

Subsidence claims are long and the early weeks set their length. Within five
working days of notification:

1. Appoint a subsidence engineer from the panel.
2. Ask the policyholder for photographs of every crack, with a rule or coin in
   shot for scale, and the dates the cracks were first noticed.
3. Establish whether any tree is within a distance of the building equal to its
   mature height, and record the species.
4. Confirm whether the property has been underpinned before, and obtain any
   previous engineer's report.

## Investigation

The engineer's first report gives a provisional cause and recommends monitoring
or immediate action. Where monitoring is recommended, the standard period is
twelve months, so that seasonal ground movement is observed across a full cycle.
Shorter monitoring gives a result that cannot be relied on.

Site investigation includes trial pits to establish foundation depth, soil
sampling to establish plasticity, and drainage tests. Drainage defects are a
common cause and are cheaper to remedy than any structural work, so test drains
early rather than late.

## Vegetation

Where vegetation is implicated and the tree is on third party land, write to the
owner setting out the evidence and asking for removal or reduction. Where the
tree is protected or is owned by a local authority, allow a longer period and
keep the correspondence, because it supports any later recovery.

## Repair

Repair follows the engineer's recommendation. Underpinning is a last resort and
is not carried out where removing the cause and allowing the ground to recover
would work. Cosmetic repair is deferred until movement has stopped.

## Communication

Contact the policyholder at least once a month, even where nothing has changed,
and say plainly that nothing has changed. Silence during a monitoring period is
the single largest source of complaints on this class.

## Excess

One excess applies for each separate subsidence event, not for each year of the
claim. Where a claim runs across more than one policy year, the excess is taken
once, in the year the damage was first notified.
